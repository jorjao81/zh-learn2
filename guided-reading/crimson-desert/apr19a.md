# Phrase Analysis: 不减当年

## Pinyin

bù jiǎn dāng nián

## Pronunciation

[bù jiǎn dāng nián](audio/minimax-tts_不减当年_Chinese__Mandarin__Male_Announcer_CE5E69D37D.mp3)

## Meaning & Usage

**不减当年** (bù jiǎn dāng nián) = **"As good as back in the day / undiminished from former years."**

This phrase is used to praise someone whose skills, vigor, beauty, or charisma have **not faded with time**. It is a highly complimentary expression, often used when an older person performs a task with the same excellence or energy they possessed in their youth. 

For example, if a retired athlete plays a game and still performs brilliantly, you would say their skill is **不减当年**. 

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

**不减当年** (bù jiǎn dāng nián): **不** (not) + **减** (reduce/diminish) + **当年** (those years / back then) = not reduced compared to those years

**不** (bù): Not, no.
**减** (jiǎn): To subtract, to reduce, to diminish, to decrease.
**当** (dāng): To be, to act as, at that time (in 当年).
**年** (nián): Year.

## Meaning & Usage

**杂碎** (zá suì) = **"Chopped entrails / giblets" OR (as an insult) "Scum / piece of trash."**

Literally, this noun refers to a culinary dish made from the chopped internal organs (heart, liver, intestines, etc.) of animals like pigs or sheep. However, in slang and media (such as movies or games), it is frequently used as a **harsh insult** to call someone worthless, despicable, or low-life—equivalent to calling someone "scum," "trash," or a "bastard."

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

**杂碎** (zá suì): **杂** (mixed/miscellaneous) + **碎** (broken/fragmented) = a mixture of fragmented parts

**杂** (zá): Mixed, miscellaneous, assorted.
**碎** (suì): Broken, fragmented, shattered, in pieces.

## Meaning & Usage

Destroying that place is the source of our power. (Sentence). A piece of lore, indicating that the act of destruction (or the destroyed place) fuels the faction's strength. Note: Linguistically, "既是" (jì shì - since it is / both) is highly likely a typo in the game's localization for "即是" (jí shì - is exactly / is precisely), which makes perfect grammatical sense: "Destroying that place is exactly the source of our power."

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

破坏那处既是我们力量之源: 破坏 (Destroying) + 那处 (that place) + 既是 (is) + 我们 (our) + 力量 (power) + 之 (of) + 源 (source) = Destroying that place is our source of power.
既是 (jì shì): Literally "since it is" or "is both". Likely a phonetic typo for 即是 (jí shì), meaning "is exactly" or "is namely".
力量之源 (lìliàng zhī yuán): Source of power. 源 (yuán - source/spring) has the water radical (氵), indicating the origin point of a stream, extended to mean the source of anything abstract like power.

# Word Analysis: 移除

## Pinyin

Yíchú

## Meaning & Usage

To remove; to clear away. (Verb). A standard UI or gameplay term, such as removing an item from a slot, clearing a debuff, or deleting a structure.

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

移除: 移 (Move) + 除 (eliminate) = Move and eliminate.
移 (yí): To move, to shift. The radical is 禾 (grain), originally relating to transplanting crops.
除 (chú): To remove, to get rid of. The radical is 阝 (mound/steps), originally relating to clearing steps or pathways.

# Word Analysis: 打孔

## Pinyin

Dǎ kǒng

## Meaning & Usage

To punch a hole; to add a socket. (Verb Object Phrase). In an RPG equipment system, this refers to adding a socket to weapons or armor so that gems, runes, or power cores can be inserted to enhance stats.

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

打孔: 打 (Make/punch) + 孔 (hole) = Make a hole.
打 (dǎ): To hit, to beat, to make. A highly versatile action verb used for many manual tasks. Features the hand radical (扌).
孔 (kǒng): Hole, aperture, socket. Character visually represents an opening.
