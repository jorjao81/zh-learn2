# Word Analysis: 嘴欠

## Pinyin

zuǐ qiàn

## Pronunciation

[zuǐ qiàn](audio/forvo_嘴欠_YvetteDC_E3C9B1775D.mp3)

## Meaning & Usage

**嘴欠** (zuǐ qiàn) = **"To have a sharp tongue / to speak thoughtlessly / to be unable to keep one's mouth shut."**

This is a **colloquial adjective** used to describe someone who **habitually says inappropriate, hurtful, or unnecessary things** — often regretting it immediately after. It captures the behavior of **speaking without thinking**, making snide remarks, or being unable to resist commenting when silence would be wiser.

The term carries a **mildly critical but often affectionate** tone. It can be used:

- **Self-deprecatingly**: Acknowledging one's own tendency to say the wrong thing.
- **Playfully**: Teasing a friend who just made an awkward comment.
- **Critically**: Describing someone whose words cause unnecessary trouble.

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

**嘴欠** (zuǐ qiàn): **嘴** (mouth) + **欠** (to lack) = the mouth lacks [proper restraint / discretion]

**嘴** (zuǐ): Mouth — the organ of speech and eating Contains the **口** (mouth) radical on the left.

**欠** (qiàn): To owe, to lack, to fall short; also: to yawn (in the word 打哈欠 dǎ hā qian)

# Sentence Analysis: 我都快闷死了

## Pinyin

## Pronunciation

[wǒ dōu kuài mēn sǐ le](audio/minimax-tts_我都快闷死了_Chinese__Mandarin__Male_Announcer_FD7A7B5E27.mp3)

## Meaning & Usage

**我都快闷死了** (wǒ dōu kuài mēn sǐ le) = **"I'm bored to death!"** / **"I'm going crazy from the stuffiness!"** / **"I'm suffocating from boredom!"**

This is an **exclamatory sentence** expressing extreme boredom or feeling stifled — either physically (in a stuffy, airless room) or psychologically (with nothing to do, nothing happening). The speaker is dramatically conveying their discomfort or restlessness.

The sentence has two common readings depending on context:

- **Physical**: "I'm suffocating from the stuffy air / heat." (闷 = mēn, first tone — stuffy, airless)
- **Psychological**: "I'm bored out of my mind." (闷 = mèn, fourth tone — bored, depressed)

In everyday speech, the first tone (mēn) is more common and the phrase is often used interchangeably for both meanings.

## Literal Translation / Breakdown

**我都快闷死了** (wǒ dōu kuài mēn sǐ le) = "I'm bored/stifled to death!"

**我** (wǒ): I, me — the first-person singular pronoun

**都** (dōu): All, both, entirely; already; used as an emphatic intensifier Contains the **阝** (city/mound) radical on the right.

**快** (kuài): Fast, quick, soon, about to, almost Contains the **忄** (heart) radical on the left.

**闷** (mēn / mèn): Stuffy, stifling, airless (mēn, first tone); bored, gloomy, depressed (mèn, fourth tone) Contains the **门** (door/gate) radical.

**死** (sǐ): To die, death; dead; as a complement: to the extreme degree ("to death") The character is a **semantic compound**.

**了** (le): Sentence-final particle signaling a **change of state** — "I have reached the point of almost dying of boredom."

# Phrase Analysis: 结实耐用

## Pinyin

jiē shi nài yòng

Note: 结 is also commonly read as jié (second tone), but in the word 结实, it is specifically first tone (jiē).

## Pronunciation

[jiē shi nài yòng](audio/minimax-tts_结实耐用_Chinese__Mandarin__News_Anchor_FC13F0F6B5.mp3)

## Meaning & Usage

**结实耐用** (jiē shi nài yòng) = **"Sturdy and durable / solid and long-lasting / well-built and hard-wearing."**

This is a **four-character descriptive phrase** used to describe objects, materials, or constructions that are **both strong in build and capable of withstanding prolonged use**. It is the go-to phrase for praising the quality and longevity of a physical item — the kind of thing you say about a well-made tool, a sturdy piece of furniture, or reliable gear.

The phrase is actually a **compound of two independent words** that are often used separately but frequently paired for emphasis:
